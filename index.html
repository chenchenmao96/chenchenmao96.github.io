Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science · Lehigh University

I study how people use AI outputs and interface capability cues to judge a system’s capabilities, humanlike qualities, and trustworthiness—and where those judgments reach their limits.

I run controlled and in-situ field experiments, build the browser extensions and experimental platforms they require, and analyze the results with structural equation modeling. A central focus is measurement validity: establishing what an instrument measures before drawing conclusions from it.

Dissertation research

Why do people move beyond evaluating what AI does to judging whether it is intelligent, humanlike, or trustworthy? My dissertation examines the relationships between these judgments and their boundaries. Study 1 provides an empirical starting point, Study 2 develops the research method, and Study 3 brings them together to test capability cues and refine the framework.

When Readability and Source Retention Diverge

Study 1 · Output evaluability and system appraisals · N = 306

In TransLingo, participants viewed source text and AI translation side by side. For simple texts, quality ratings tracked audited differences in source retention; for the tested complex prose, outputs with sharply different source overlap received nearly identical ratings. Seeing the source did not ensure that participants could evaluate what the translation retained.

Perceived quality, perceived intelligence, agency-oriented attribution, and task-performance trust were related. Trust was associated with stated disclosure willingness, but condition differences in trust did not correspond to reliable differences in disclosure willingness.

These results describe appraisal relationships, not an established causal sequence. Rendering conditions coincided with collection periods, and text complexity was confounded with genre and other source properties. Disclosure measures captured willingness rather than behavior.

Outer Limits

Study 2 · Controlled experimentation within an existing platform · N = 219 · Accepted at CSSSA 2026

Moving from a standalone text tool to an AI assistant within a platform requires control over what participants encounter while retaining the platform interface. Outer Limits is an open-source Chrome extension and backend that replaces content inside Old Reddit, records interactions, and contains constructed content and configured write actions.

Validation combined ART ANOVAs, exploratory equivalence tests at a d = ±0.50 bound, and qualitative coding. It assessed perceptual fidelity within the tested materials and measures, rather than establishing natural behavior or broad ecological validity. This infrastructure supports the platform-embedded experiment in Study 3.

How Far Do Capability Cues Travel?

Study 3 · Capability cues and differentiated trust · N = 270

A randomized 2 × 2 experiment through Outer Limits tested displays of functional breadth and explanation. Displaying multiple functions increased perceived multifunctionality—the only randomized main effect to survive correction for multiple testing. Broader effects on intelligence, anthropomorphism, and trust were not reliably supported in this study.

In the analysis of measured perceptions, perceived intelligence was associated with ability, integrity, and benevolence trust. After accounting for perceived intelligence, anthropomorphism’s additional association was specific to benevolence: whether the assistant was seen as caring about the user’s interests.

The findings distinguish recognizing a capability cue from changing a broader judgment. Associations among concurrently measured perceptions do not establish a causal process, and the experimental results do not rule out effects in other settings.
